# SpaceNova — Historical timeline

**Revision:** 0.1 · 2026-11-26

**Status:** the project owner approved the broad historical timeline on 2026-11-26. The 500-year interval, eight historical milestones/periods below, and their broad historical themes are accepted foundations. Explanatory details are working interpretations; named expeditions, exact incident dates, individual political histories and later campaign durations remain open.

**Related:** [LORE.md](LORE.md), [CAMPAIGN.md](CAMPAIGN.md), [FACTIONS.md](FACTIONS.md), [CHARACTERS.md](CHARACTERS.md), [First Run](../story-first-run.md).

## 1. Historical reference

Year 0 is the first successful human interstellar crossing from SOL to Alpha Centauri. Year 500 is the opening of First Run, five centuries later.

These relative years are an authoring convention, not an approved in-universe calendar name or Gregorian date. Period boundaries below are broad historical markers, not simultaneous changes throughout civilization. Adjacent periods share boundary years; a local event near a boundary can reflect either historical trend.

The four overarching ages established in LORE.md remain intact. The additional periods subdivide the Age of Expansion and describe the transition into the Age of Fracture; they do not introduce competing calendars or replace the four ages.

## 2. Approved historical framework

| Relative year(s) | Period | Overarching age | Defining development |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before 0 | The Solar Age | Solar, followed by early Discovery | Colonization of SOL, planetary independence and commercial organizations; discovery of the anomaly beyond Pluto precedes the first crossing. |
| 0 | The First Crossing | Discovery | Humanity successfully traverses the SOL wormhole and reaches Alpha Centauri. |
| 1–100 | The Frontier Century | Expansion | Early settlements, dangerous expeditions and development of passage navigation technology. |
| 100–250 | The Great Expansion | Expansion | Additional systems become accessible; interstellar commerce and independent governments flourish. |
| 250–400 | The Age of Prosperity | Expansion | Mature trade networks, technological advancement and distinct regional cultures. |
| 400–480 | Growing Tensions | Late Expansion | Commercial monopolies, territorial disputes and disagreements over passage regulation; ideological foundations of the Directorate emerge. |
| 480–500 | The Fracture | Fracture | Disappearances, piracy, shortages and unexplained passage instability threaten established civilization. |
| 500 | First Run | Fracture | The player begins as a Helios courier in SOL. |

## 3. Historical periods

### Before Year 0 — The Solar Age and early Discovery

Humanity establishes orbital habitats, industry, refineries and transportation within SOL before interstellar travel exists. Independent planetary authorities and commercial institutions develop to meet local needs. Earth remains the ancestral home, but does not acquire permanent authority over every settlement.

Surveyors discover a gravitational anomaly beyond Pluto. Early attempts to traverse it cause losses and structural damage, including damage that shields cannot prevent.

**Open for future writing:** dates of SOL settlement, individual institutions' founding histories, discovery expedition, lost vessels and the interval between discovery and the successful crossing.

### Year 0 — The First Crossing

The first successful human interstellar transit reaches Alpha Centauri through the passage beyond Pluto. This is the defining historical reference point.

The crossing establishes that interstellar movement is possible; it does not instantly establish reliable travel, communication, regular commerce or a complete map of the network.

**Open:** vessel, expedition name, crew, exact discovery circumstances, and early records. These are useful subjects for later historical discoveries and optional quests.

### Years 1–100 — The Frontier Century

Expeditions and early settlers face hazardous passage conditions and incomplete navigation knowledge. Generations of engineers and surveyors begin developing practical procedures and specialized instruments. Small settlements depend heavily on transport, maintenance and supply.

**Potential story material:** early expedition logs, missing survey records, pioneer communities, recovered instruments and historical disagreements over the first routes. No named disaster or settlement founding date is established by this outline.

### Years 100–250 — The Great Expansion

New passages are charted and tested. Stations and settlements expand around viable routes. Commercial carriers, local governments and specialist organizations grow to support movement between systems.

Political independence and economic interdependence develop together. A remote settlement can govern itself while relying on imported equipment or medicine.

**Potential story material:** founding charters, early commercial agreements, disputes over infrastructure and historical freight routes. The order and dates in which the 29 systems were historically reached are deliberately unspecified.

### Years 250–400 — The Age of Prosperity

Interstellar trade becomes an established part of ordinary life. Local economies, institutions and cultural identities mature. Technical improvements make conventional travel and passage support more dependable, without eliminating the physical limitations in LORE.md.

**Potential story material:** commercial archives, engineering legacies, family histories, cultural traditions and agreements that still matter at Year 500. Prosperity need not have been universal or uninterrupted.

### Years 400–480 — Growing Tensions

Disputes over economic concentration, territorial authority, access to passages and safety standards intensify. The benefits of intersystem trade become unevenly distributed. The idea that a centralized passage authority could provide greater security gains traction among some people.

These tensions form the ideological background of the proposed Directorate. The organization itself has no approved founding date, founding incident, membership list or public identity in this period.

**Potential story material:** disputed trade agreements, failed coordination efforts, testimony, legal records and competing interpretations of historical events. Do not establish a specific war or atrocity without review.

### Years 480–500 — The Fracture

During the final approximately twenty years before First Run, disappearing vessels, piracy, route disruptions, supply shortages and political distrust become defining pressures. Different systems experience those pressures differently; the period boundary is not the date of a single confirmed disaster.

At the opening of the game, most people recognize local consequences rather than a proven interstellar conspiracy. A merchant worries about a shipment; an engineer worries about a vessel's damage; a dispatcher worries about missing crews.

The campaign's proposed hardline Directorate operations exploit or manufacture some of this instability. Do not attribute every loss or dispute to the Directorate, or establish an exact start date for deliberate interference before the relevant evidence and chronology are reviewed.

**Potential story material:** contemporary missing-vessel records, contested contracts, witnesses, procurement anomalies and competing explanations for outages.

### Year 500 — First Run

The player begins their first solo Helios run in SOL. The existing prologue is the authoritative account of their personal experience:

- Accept the three-crate medical consignment from Dispatcher Halden Ross.
- Encounter Vesper-02, respond to its distress and resolve the Corsair-17 encounter.
- Recover the verified navigation record through surrender and have Mara inspect the military-grade weapons damage.
- Report the evidence to Juno and complete the Mars delivery.
- Receive the existing 500-credit reward and return invitation.

See the [implemented prologue design](../story-first-run.md) and [playthrough](../story-first-run-playthrough.md) for exact sequencing, retry behavior and authoritative triggers. The timeline does not alter these rules or create additional mission effects.

Chapter 1, Leaked Routes, follows the prologue. The rest of the 29-system campaign is a planned narrative sequence, not an approved calendar of future global events.

## 4. Chronology versus multiplayer progression

Distinguish three forms of time:

| Time domain | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Historical chronology | Common world history before the player enters the game. The approved periods are shared narrative foundations. |
| Shared simulation time | Server-owned live world, traffic, trade and station activity. Personal story completion does not advance the historical year or rewrite the shared universe. |
| Personal campaign chronology | Each pilot's verified sequence of accepted missions, encounters, discoveries and decisions. Two pilots can occupy the same station at different story stages. |

The Year 500 opening is the narrative setting, not a rule that every real player must start on the same literal in-game date. No mapping from wall-clock time to fictional calendar years or mandatory duration for later acts is approved.

The campaign's sequential system unlocks are pilot progression, not the historical order of human discovery. Established interstellar societies already exist in the lore. SOL remains the only currently playable system, and completing First Run alone does not unlock Alpha Centauri.

Historical additions should enrich the world without changing permanent NPC identities, home locations, services or established vessel routes. See [CHARACTERS.md](CHARACTERS.md). A historic character can appear in records or recollections without requiring a new persistent actor. Personal narrative developments remain scoped to the eligible pilot or authorized encounter.

## 5. Evidence and revelation

The historical truth available to writers is not necessarily what the pilot knows. Historical records can be incomplete or contested; new scripts must distinguish a verified fact, a character's interpretation, and an unconfirmed rumor.

Preserve the campaign revelation ladder:

- First Run establishes the leaked navigation record and unusual weapons damage.
- SOL investigates the leak and the supply chain without confirming a culprit prematurely.
- The proposed Directorate identification occurs at Tau Ceti.
- The ancient engineering of the network is substantially revealed in Act IV.
- The Architects' origins, identity, era and ultimate fate remain open.

The Architects predate human expansion under the accepted ancient-engineering premise, but no date, age, extinction event or surviving population is established. Do not assign them a human historical year without explicit review.

## 6. Adding historical events

Add a dated event only when it serves a reviewed narrative, institution, character or world-building need. Record:

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Event ID and title | Stable reference for future documents |
| Relative date or range | Use a range when the exact year is unknown |
| System and location | Respect the existing catalogue; mark new places as proposed |
| Participants | Existing or explicitly proposed factions and characters |
| Event and consequences | Establish why this matters to later history |
| Knowledge and evidence | Distinguish public history, restricted records, rumors and author-only truth |
| Status | Proposed or approved; implementation tracked separately |
| Dependencies | Related lore, character, faction, and mission references |

Before accepting a new event, check that it fits the five-century interval, technological limits, character continuity, institutional dependencies and established revelation order. A historical reference must not silently introduce a new live feature.

## 7. Deliberate gaps

The following remain available for future development:

- Absolute calendar year and in-universe date format.
- The first expedition's name, ship, crew and discovery records.
- Dates of individual system discoveries and settlements.
- Institutional founding dates, historic agreements, political disputes and wars.
- Directorate founding, leadership and the exact beginning of its covert operations.
- Birth dates, careers and personal histories of individual NPCs.
- The Architects' chronology and fate.
- Exact duration of the active campaign and the post-campaign historical era.

Do not fill these gaps simply to make a complete-looking timeline. The approved framework should allow years of additional story content without contradicting the original game.
